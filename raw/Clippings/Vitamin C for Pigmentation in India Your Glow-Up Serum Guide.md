## What Does Vitamin C Actually Do for Pigmentation?

Pigmentation happens when melanocytes — the cells that give your skin its gorgeous depth — overproduce melanin in certain spots, usually triggered by sun, inflammation, or hormones. Vitamin C steps in as a multitasker. It scavenges the free radicals generated by UV and pollution before they can spark more melanin, and it gently down-regulates tyrosinase, the enzyme that kicks melanin production into overdrive. The result over weeks is marks that look faded and a tone that reads more uniform.

Vitamin C serum helps fade pigmentation on Indian skin by acting as an antioxidant that supports a brighter, more even-looking tone over time when paired with daily SPF. It neutralises UV-triggered free radicals, calms excess melanin activity, and boosts collagen, so post-acne marks and tan lines gradually appear softer and more even across the complexion.

**Vitamin C Spotlight:** INCI names include Ascorbic Acid (the pure, potent form) and gentler derivatives like Sodium Ascorbyl Phosphate. Its molecular action is electron-donating antioxidant defence plus tyrosinase inhibition, and its skin benefit is a visibly brighter, more even-looking tone with support for firmer, collagen-rich skin.

## What Mistakes Waste Your Vitamin C?

Even the best **even tone serum in India** can't save you from routine sabotage. Here are the slip-ups that quietly cancel out your glow — and how to dodge them:

- **Skipping SPF** — This is the biggest one. Fresh sun exposure re-triggers the exact pigmentation you're fading, so no sunscreen means running on a treadmill.
- **Over-layering acids** — Stacking vitamin C with strong exfoliating acids or high-strength retinol in one sitting can irritate your barrier, and irritation causes more pigmentation. Alternate them across morning and night instead.
- **Storing it in sunlight** — Vitamin C oxidises when exposed to light and air. If your serum turns deep orange or brown, its potency has dropped. Keep it capped, cool, and away from your sunny windowsill.
- **Impatience** — Quitting at week three because "nothing's happening." Pigmentation fades on a monthly, not weekly, clock.
- **Ignoring the neck and hands** — Tan lines don't respect boundaries, so bring your serum and SPF below the jaw too.

Cosmetic scientists note that consistency beats concentration every time — a stable serum used daily outperforms a super-strength one you use twice a week.

### How do I know if my vitamin C serum has gone bad?

Your vitamin C serum has likely oxidised and gone bad when it turns from clear or pale yellow to a dark orange, brown, or amber shade. Oxidised vitamin C loses its brightening power and can actually cause irritation or stain the skin, so that colour shift is your cue to toss it. Other warning signs include a strange metallic smell or a change in texture. To slow oxidation, store your serum in a cool, dark place, keep the cap tightly sealed, and choose formulas in opaque or dark glass bottles.
