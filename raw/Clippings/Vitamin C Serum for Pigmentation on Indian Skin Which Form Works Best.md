## Why Vitamin C Is the Gold Standard for Indian Hyperpigmentation

India's year-round UV intensity, combined with the higher baseline melanin in Fitzpatrick IV–V skin, makes hyperpigmentation one of the most common skin concerns on the subcontinent. Sun exposure, hormonal fluctuations, and post-inflammatory responses all trigger excess melanin — and those dark spots linger far longer on deeper skin tones than they do on lighter ones.

### How melanin overproduction causes dark spots on Indian skin

Melanin is produced by melanocytes in response to UV exposure and inflammation. In darker skin tones, melanocytes are naturally more reactive — they produce pigment more readily and disperse it unevenly, which is why a single pimple or sun patch on Indian skin can leave a mark that lasts weeks, sometimes months. This post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) is especially stubborn at Fitzpatrick IV–V.

### What vitamin C does at the cellular level

Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid in its purest form) works by inhibiting tyrosinase — the enzyme that triggers melanin production. It also neutralises free radicals generated by UV exposure before they can activate pigmentation pathways. Research published in the *Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology* found that topical Vitamin C applied consistently over 16 weeks produced significant reduction in hyperpigmentation across Fitzpatrick III–V skin types, without bleaching or stripping the skin's natural tone.

### Stability in India's heat and humidity

Ascorbic acid is notoriously unstable. At temperatures above 25°C — which describes most Indian cities for the better part of the year — it oxidises rapidly, turning the serum yellow or brown and rendering it ineffective (or worse, pro-inflammatory). Yuzu-derived Vitamin C retains its antioxidant activity under these conditions, making it a far more practical choice for daily use across India.

### Skin tolerance on darker Indian skin tones

Yuzu-derived Vitamin C is better suited for Indian skin in hot climates than ascorbic acid because it is more stable in heat, less likely to cause irritation on darker skin tones, and still delivers visible brightening results on melanin-rich skin. Cosmetic scientists note that the lower acidity of yuzu derivatives means far fewer stinging or flushing reactions — a real concern at Fitzpatrick IV–V.

### Potency vs. gentleness trade-off

Ascorbic acid acts directly and aggressively — which is effective but also the reason it causes sensitivity. Yuzu Vitamin C works through a broader antioxidant and brightening cascade, including flavonoids and citric compounds native to the yuzu fruit. The results are visible and steady without the barrier disruption that higher-concentration L-AA can cause on Indian skin.

### Which form suits beginners?

If you are new to actives, yuzu-derived Vitamin C is the safer entry point. Ascorbic acid at effective concentrations (10–20%) requires careful pH management and a skin barrier that is already in good shape. Yuzu formulations are typically pH-neutral and can be layered with other actives — including Niacinamide — without risking the purging or irritation that beginners often blame on "sensitive skin."

## Other Vitamin C Derivatives Worth Knowing

Two other derivatives are worth a quick mention — not to complicate your routine, but because you'll see them on ingredient lists and it helps to know what you're working with.

### Sodium ascorbyl phosphate

**Sodium ascorbyl phosphate (SAP)** is a water-soluble, stable derivative that converts to ascorbic acid on the skin. It's gentler than L-AA, works well at higher pH, and has shown particular promise in studies for acne-prone skin due to an antimicrobial secondary action. It's a solid middle-ground option — more stable than pure ascorbic acid, though slightly less potent than yuzu-derived forms in terms of antioxidant breadth.

### Tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate (oil-soluble C)

This oil-soluble form penetrates lipid-rich skin layers more effectively and is exceptionally stable. It tends to appear in richer serums and moisturisers rather than lightweight water-based formulas. For Indian skin prone to congestion, it works best in low concentrations layered under SPF rather than as a standalone serum step.

**Yuzu Spotlight:** Citrus junos (INCI: Citrus Junos Fruit Extract). Rich in hesperidin, naringenin, and ascorbic acid precursors, yuzu inhibits tyrosinase activity and neutralises UV-triggered free radicals. Its flavonoid profile provides antioxidant action beyond Vitamin C alone, making it especially effective against the chronic, low-grade oxidative stress that drives pigmentation in tropical climates.
