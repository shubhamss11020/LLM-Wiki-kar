## What Should 'Cruelty-Free & Vegan' Mean on a Budget?

For ingredient-aware Gen Z, "cruelty-free" and "vegan" carry specific, checkable meanings — not just pretty packaging buzzwords. Here is what each claim actually demands from a formula.

### No animal testing

Cruelty-free means no animal testing happens at any stage, from raw material to finished lipstick. Every SUGAR product clears this bar, full stop.

### Carmine-free for true vegan status

Carmine is a red pigment crushed from cochineal insects, and it hides in countless red lipsticks and blushes. **Carmine-free makeup** swaps it for synthetic or mineral pigments, which is why our reds and berries qualify as vegan.

Cruelty-free means a product was never tested on animals at any stage, while vegan means it contains zero animal-derived ingredients. They're not the same thing. A lipstick can be cruelty-free but still sneak in beeswax, carmine (crushed beetles, yes really), or lanolin — which makes it non-vegan. And a product can technically be vegan but still tested on animals somewhere down the supply chain. For the full ethical win, look for both labels together.
